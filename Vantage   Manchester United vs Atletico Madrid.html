Beaten in 47 seconds, then undone by a red card and a rebound

Atlético Madrid scored after 47 seconds when a ball over the top found Pablo Barrios, who finished from Alex Baena's pass. Manchester United answered with the better chances but missed all four of their big ones, then lost Leny Yoro to a 74' red card for a last-man foul on Jonathan David. Substitute Ademola Lookman made it 2-0 at 79' from the rebound after Lammens saved David's shot.

The numbers lean Atlético's way: possession 58-42, shots 11-9, on target 5-4 and expected goals 1.42 to 0.98. The gap is shot quality. United's four shots on target were worth 0.77 expected goals on target, against 2.33 for Atlético's five.

Where United led: they won 64% of the duels, made 21 tackles to Atlético's 5, completed 9 of 14 dribbles and won 5 corners to 2. They created 9 chances to 7 and had four big chances to Atlético's two. Four big chances missed is their most in a Champions League game since September 2012.

The key question

Why couldn't United score from four big chances? What the stats can and can't say.

Possible

Shot quality, not volume

United's four shots on target were worth 0.77 xGOT, about 0.19 each. Atlético's five were worth 2.33. Oblak made four saves, but he faced shots that were not hard to stop.

Possible

The big chances fell to the wrong moments

Rashford, Mbeumo, Fernandes and Baleba each had a big chance. Baleba's was a header at a corner, straight into Oblak's arms from an unmarked position at 25'.

Early, not late

It wasn't the red card

Three of the four big chances came before half-time. The red card explains the last 20 minutes, not the missed chances.

Possible

Crossing didn't help

United attempted 13 crosses and found a team-mate once (7.7%). Atlético made one accurate cross from five. Neither side found much from wide.

Can't test yet

Where the chances came from

There is no shot map. To know whether the chances came from the plan or from transitions, log the origin, the pass type and the body part for every shot.
